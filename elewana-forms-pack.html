A10 · Section 8.6.4

Bomb Threat — Questions to Ask

Keep calm, pretend difficulty hearing, keep the caller talking. If the caller seems agreeable to further conversation, ask:

  • When will it go off? (Certain hour / time remaining)
  • Where is it located? (building, floor, room, etc.)
  • What kind of bomb is it?
  • What does it look like, what kind of package?
  • How do you know so much about the bomb?
  • What will make it explode?
  • Did you place the bomb? Yes / No / Why?
  • What is your name and address?

If the building is occupied

Inform the caller that detonation could cause injury or death.

After the call: notify your supervisor immediately. Use an alternative phone or means to call Security, the Emergency Coordinator, or the General Manager, and relay information about the call.

Did the caller appear familiar with the Hotel/Lodge or building (by their description of the bomb location)? Write out the message in its entirety and any other comments on a separate sheet of paper and attach to this checklist.

General Manager (IC) and Security — then police

  • Head Office updated

  • Head Office updated

  • All Clear declared or evacuation ordered

  • Team debriefed · report submitted
  • Equipment checked, replenished, drill records updated

Quick Protocol

  • Keep the patient calm and as still as possible; immobilise the bitten limb with a splint.
  • Do NOT cut the wound, suck out venom, or apply a tourniquet.
  • Apply a light compression bandage above the wound.
  • If the patient stops breathing, start CPR immediately.
  • If it's safe, photograph the snake to help with identification — never try to catch it.

Species-by-species risks and safety tips. Post at guide briefings and the Command Post.

AnimalKey riskSafety tips
ElephantCharges if surprised or provokedStay quiet and still. Retreat slowly behind hard cover if there's space. Never block the path or a calf.
Lion / LeopardNocturnal stalking, close-quarters riskNever walk alone; keep lights on; report sightings. If an encounter is imminent, back away facing the animal — don't run.

Monkey / BaboonAggressive when fedNever feed; keep windows and doors closed; secure food.
